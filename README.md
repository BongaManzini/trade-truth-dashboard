## 🔬 About This Project

Whenever political news breaks about South Africa's international relations, people often argue about foreign aid and trade deals without looking at the actual numbers.

I pulled the official trade statistics from the South African Revenue Service (SARS) customs database and the US Department of Commerce. South Africa actually exports more goods to the US than it imports from them—giving South Africa a multi-billion dollar trade export surplus! We export platinum group metals, cars manufactured in Pretoria and Durban, and citrus fruit from Limpopo under the duty-free AGOA trade agreement.

I built this dashboard to make trade data easy for students, journalists, and citizens to explore.

## The 11 Questions: The Facts Behind The Project

### 1. Who is this for?
- Business students, exporters, journalists, and citizens interested in South Africa's global economic standing.

### 2. Where does this focus on?
- South Africa and bilateral trade with the United States.

### 3. Why did I build it?
- Public debates on foreign policy often confuse foreign aid with trade. South Africa is a major trade partner, not an aid-dependent country.

### 4. How does the dashboard work?
- An interactive chart where you can filter exports by sector (minerals, automotive, agriculture) and view duty-free AGOA trade volumes.

### 5. When does this cover?
- Trade data from 2000 to 2026.

### 6. The Problem I Noticed
- Many people think the US gives billions in cash aid to South Africa. In reality, the economic relationship is based on trade surplus and AGOA duty-free lines.

### 7. Where I Got The Data
- SARS Customs monthly trade statistics and US Department of Commerce trade reports.

### 8. Why I Trusted This Source
- SARS is the statutory authority that tracks every single shipping container entering or leaving South African ports.

### 9. How I Built It
- I parsed the monthly export/import CSV files, calculated net balances across sectors, and built responsive charts with Chart.js.

### 10. What I Hope To Achieve
- A transparent economic reference that anyone can consult during international trade discussions.

### 11. Why This Matters In Real Life
- Real trade data shows which local jobs (like car manufacturing workers or citrus farmers) depend on trade agreements.
